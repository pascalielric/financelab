03Forwards, carry and fit quality

The forward comes from put call parity, C − P = DF × (F − K), using the median across strikes near the money; the discount factor DF comes from the 13 week Treasury bill rate. Carry is the annualised log ratio of forward to spot: the interest rate minus the dividend yield the options imply. Fit error is the root mean square gap between market and SVI volatilities.

04Methodology

Pricing

Black Scholes Merton with a continuous dividend yield gives the closed form price and Greeks of a European option. The Cox Ross Rubinstein binomial tree builds the share price as a lattice of up and down moves; working back from expiry it gives the same European price as the number of steps grows, and allows early exercise at every node, which values American options. An American put is worth more than a European one because exercising early earns interest on the strike.

Implied volatility is the volatility that makes Black's formula on the forward match the market price. It is solved by Newton's method on vega with a bisection safeguard, so it always converges.

The surface

The data pipeline downloads option chains from Yahoo Finance every weekday. Only out of the money options are used (puts below the forward, calls above), because they are the most traded and carry no early exercise premium. Prices are the bid and ask midpoint; when the book is empty, as outside US trading hours, the last trade is used and the fit is noisier.

Each maturity is fitted with the raw SVI (stochastic volatility inspired) parameterisation of Gatheral: total variance w(k) = a + b(ρ(k − m) + √((k − m)² + σ²)), where k is log moneyness. It has five parameters, captures skew and curvature, and is constrained so variance never turns negative. Yahoo's own implied volatilities are shown on the smile only as a cross check.
