Method

Geometric Brownian motion. Each asset follows dS/S = μ dt + σ dW. Weekly log returns are drawn as (μ − ½σ²)Δt + L·z·√Δt, where L is the Cholesky factor of the annualised covariance matrix and z a vector of independent standard normal draws, so simulated assets keep their historical correlations.

Block bootstrap. Instead of assuming normality, the simulation resamples actual five day blocks of joint historical returns. This preserves fat tails, skew and short term clustering that GBM misses, at the price of being limited to what happened in the sample.

Rebalancing. The portfolio is rebalanced to its target weights every week, so the portfolio return of a step is the weighted average of the simple asset returns.

Reading the result. The fan shows the 5th to 95th percentile band. The left tail matters more than the median: horizon VaR and CVaR summarise it, and the probability of loss shows how often the path ends below the start.
