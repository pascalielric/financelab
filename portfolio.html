03Value at risk and expected shortfall

VaR (value at risk) is the loss not exceeded with the chosen confidence over the horizon. CVaR (conditional VaR, also called expected shortfall) is the average loss in the cases beyond VaR. Multi day figures scale the one day estimate by the square root of time, except Monte Carlo, which simulates the full path.

05Stress tests

Each scenario applies a market shock scaled by every position's beta to the selected benchmark. It ignores second order effects such as correlation spikes and liquidity, so real losses in a crisis are typically larger for low beta assets than this suggests.

07Methodology

Returns. Daily log returns from adjusted closing prices on dates common to all holdings. Annualisation uses 252 trading days.

Parametric VaR. Portfolio value × z × σp × √h with σp = √(w′Σw), assuming normal returns and zero mean over short horizons.

Historical VaR. The empirical percentile of the portfolio's daily return series with current weights. No distribution is assumed.

Monte Carlo VaR. 20'000 correlated return paths generated with a Cholesky decomposition of the covariance matrix.

Risk attribution. Component VaR of position i is wi × marginal VaRi, where marginal VaR is z × (Σw)i / σp. Components add up to total parametric VaR.

Ratios. Sharpe uses total volatility; Sortino uses downside deviation below the risk free rate; Treynor uses beta; Calmar divides return by maximum drawdown; Omega is the ratio of gains to losses around the risk free rate; M² rescales the portfolio to benchmark volatility.

Optimisation. Mean variance optimisation (Markowitz): for each level of risk aversion the optimiser finds the mix with the best trade off between expected return and variance, fully invested, without short sales and within the position cap. Tracing 40 levels gives the frontier. Options A and B are solved directly for the current risk and the current return. Expected returns by default follow the capital asset pricing model (CAPM), with a 5.5% equity risk premium. The solver was checked against a quadratic programming reference.

Limits. Two to three years of history understate tail risk; correlations rise in crises; VaR says nothing about losses beyond the threshold, which is why CVaR and stress tests are shown next to it.
